‘EAL’ to ‘BML’: Announcing our positive change in terminology to an ‘asset-based’ approach to bilingualism and multilingualism

We’ve made a positive decision to switch our use of the term ‘EAL’ (English as an Additional Language) to ‘BML’ (Bilingual/Multilingual Learner). Why are we doing this? Our work with schools strongly indicates that there can be confusion over teachers’ and parents’ understanding of ‘EAL’ as encompassing the full range of English language proficiency; it is too often incorrectly used as interchangeable with ‘New to English’. In addition, the term ‘EAL’ places the English language as the focus.

Of course, our job in school is to develop the social and academic English needed for all pupils to ‘belong, learn and thrive’ (our new tagline!). However, research from Jim Cummins clearly shows that the ‘Common Underlying Proficiency’ (CUP) in a learner’s first language/s is a firm foundation on which to build additional languages, such as English in UK schools. We wish to place the superpower of bilingualism or multilingualism at the heart of an asset-based descriptor – hence, we’ll be predominantly using ‘BML’ (Bilingual/Multilingual Learner) on our website and in our work. Will you do the same in your school or organisation? Or perhaps you already do!

How can schools in England fund school improvement to benefit Bilingual/Multilingual Learners (BMLs)?

Are you accessing accurate funding from the DfE to support your BMLs (previously referred to as ‘pupils with English as an Additional Language’)? The DfE’s School Census (October) is coming up soon but the BML/EAL funding factor can appear hidden. It’s crucial that data for the ‘Language code’ [used for funding] item is populated accurately. To do so, the understanding of ‘First Language’ is essential. Many schools are missing out on significant BML/EAL funding due to them because of incomplete or incorrect data reporting. Our Better Bilingual Advice Sheet provides information to help you. 

A bilingual blog in English & Azerbaijani by Turan (Year 7 Student, Bristol)

“One day, I wish to celebrate Novruz in my home country, Azerbaijan, with all my relatives—especially my grandma and grandpa. I’ve never been in Azerbaijan during springtime, so I’ve never experienced Novruz there. But we always celebrate it wherever we are, keeping our traditions alive. I know about semeni, and I really enjoy the egg-fighting tradition, even though I’m not a big fan of sweets. Still, I know that baklava, shekerbura, and qogal are the special treats for this holiday. I also love having guests. Even though we’re not in our home country, celebrating Novruz with friends here still makes me feel happy and connected to our culture.”

Bahar vaxtı heç vaxt Azərbaycanda olmamışam və orada Novruzun necə keçirildiyini hiss etməmişəm. Amma biz harada yaşasaq da, bu bayramı həmişə qeyd edir, adət-ənənələrimizi yaşatmağa çalışırıq. Online CPD – via MS Teams

‘New to English’ Induction CPD Programme:
This two-session online CPD introduces schools to the Better Bilingual ‘New to English’ Induction Programme, a structured 6–8 week intervention designed to help newly arrived pupils rapidly develop survival English and feel included.

Multilingualism (EAL) Champions CPD Programme:
The Multilingualism (EAL) Champions programme is an online CPD course designed to build whole-school capacity for multilingual education and Equity, Diversity, Inclusion and Belonging (EDIB).

In-Person CPD – kindly hosted by our corporate partner, Burges Salmon (Bristol)

Adaptive Teaching for Bi- & Multilingual Learners (BMLs): This in-person CPD session focuses on adaptive teaching approaches to improve attainment for bi- and multilingual learners, particularly those working at English proficiency Bands C and D.

Summer Network Meeting - ‘Multilingual Motivators’ Launch: This twilight network event launches our Multilingual Motivators initiative, a volunteer programme designed to celebrate pupils’ first languages and strengthen their sense of belonging. This initiative, sponsored by Burges Salmon, aims to increase pride in multilingualism while accelerating English language development and academic success.
